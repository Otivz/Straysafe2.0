 CHAPTER 1 PROJECT RATIONALE Introduction The streets of a modern community serve as the silent stage for a growing animal welfare and public health concern marked by the increasing presence of stray animals. In the Philippines, the sight of wandering dogs and cats, commonly referred to as aspins and puspins, has become a familiar reality that reflects broader challenges in animal welfare management, community safety, and public health governance. These animals, whether abandoned by owners or born on the streets, often endure hunger, disease, harsh environmental conditions, and the constant risk of injury. Beyond the welfare concerns affecting the animals themselves, unmanaged stray populations contribute to the spread of zoonotic diseases, environmental sanitation issues, and safety risks within communities. Although the Animal Welfare Act of 1998, as amended by Republic Act No. 10631, establishes a legal framework for the humane treatment and control of animals, implementation varies considerably across localities, and many communities continue to rely on informal reporting mechanisms such as Facebook posts and barangay Messenger group chats. While these platforms allow rapid dissemination of information, they are not designed for incident management and provide no mechanism for tracking rescue outcomes or maintaining historical records. The development of STRAY-SAFE: Smart Stray Animal Reporting and Rescue System is motivated by the need to address these interconnected challenges through a structured and technology-driven approach. The study seeks to bridge the gap between concerned citizens, rescue organizations, subdivision leaders, and local government units (LGUs) by transforming fragmented and informal reporting practices into an organized web-based platform capable of supporting efficient rescue coordination and evidence-based decision-making.

 At the core of the STRAY-SAFE initiative is its alignment with the institutional identity of Dr. Yanga's Colleges, Inc. (DYCI). The study is guided by the DYCIan core values of MAGIS and PARAYA. Through the spirit of MAGIS, or Magis Quam Schola, Familia (More than a school, a family), the researchers aspire to become more for others by extending compassion and service to both the community and its animals. Complementing this is the value of PARAYA, which signifies the interconnectedness of Pamayanan, Paaralan, and Parokya (Community, School, and Parish). This principle emphasizes collaborative social responsibility and highlights the role of educational institutions in addressing community concerns through meaningful and sustainable initiatives. Furthermore, the study supports the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), particularly SDG 3: Good Health and Well-being, through the reduction of zoonotic disease risks, and SDG 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities, through the promotion of safer, healthier, and more responsive local communities. By improving the efficiency of stray animal reporting and rescue coordination, the proposed system contributes to broader efforts aimed at strengthening public health preparedness and community resilience. The urgency of these concerns is evident in national health data, which indicate that approximately four million animal bite cases are recorded annually in the Philippines, with more than 76 percent of recorded human rabies cases during the early months of 2026 linked to unvaccinated stray animals. These statistics are directly reflected in the local setting of this study. Santa Maria, Bulacan, where Barangay San Vicente is located, was identified by the Bulacan Provincial Health Office (PHO) as one of only three municipalities in the province with the highest number of confirmed human rabies deaths in 2023, recording three fatalities — placing it alongside San Jose del Monte and Pandi in a province that logged 53,013 animal bite cases and 24 human rabies deaths that year, representing a four-percent increase from the previous year. 

=== PDF PAGE 4 ===
 Within this municipality, Barangay San Vicente has experienced substantial population growth, increasing from 24,451 residents in 2020 to 33,691 residents in 2024 — a 37.8 percent increase in only four years. As one of the fastest-growing residential communities in Santa Maria, the barangay has witnessed the rapid expansion of subdivisions and housing developments, resulting in increased interaction between residents and stray animals. Yet the systems responsible for managing stray animal incidents have not evolved at the same pace as the community itself. An interview conducted with the Barangay Captain of San Vicente confirmed that the current reporting process relies entirely on informal channels, with no centralized system for tracking incidents or maintaining historical records. Reports submitted through Facebook or Messenger are manually processed through multiple layers of coordination before a rescue is initiated, resulting in delayed response times, undetected duplicate reports, inefficient resource allocation, and a significant accountability gap in which citizens receive little to no visibility into the outcome of their reports. These deficiencies prevent public health authorities from making data-driven decisions regarding vaccine allocation, rescue deployment, and preventive intervention planning. Despite the growing body of research on digital animal welfare systems, a significant gap remains within the context of Philippine local governance. Existing foreign platforms demonstrate the effectiveness of digital reporting, location-based monitoring, and rescue coordination technologies, but were developed within governance environments that differ substantially from the barangay-based administrative structure of the Philippines. Local platforms have likewise contributed to shelter digitization and pet adoption management, yet remain focused on companion animal services rather than community-based rescue reporting and coordination. None of the reviewed systems fully integrates AI-assisted animal validation, duplicate report detection, heatmap visualization, subdivision-level verification, and barangay-centered 

=== PDF PAGE 5 ===
 coordination within a single platform tailored to Philippine local government operations. A detailed review of these existing platforms is presented in the Review of Related Literature and Studies that follows. To address these limitations, this study proposes STRAY-SAFE: Smart Stray Animal Reporting and Rescue System, a web-based platform designed to support community-based stray animal reporting, verification, rescue coordination, post-rescue animal welfare management, and public health monitoring. The system transforms informal reporting practices into a structured, multi-tiered workflow that carries a report from citizen submission, through subdivision-level validation and escalation, to barangay-level dispatch and field rescue, and finally to administrator-level oversight. By incorporating AI-assisted animal validation, geolocation-based report submission, duplicate report detection, real-time case tracking and notifications, stray density heatmap visualization, a Pet QR Registration and Management feature, holding facility monitoring, ownership claim verification, adoption management, community and hazard alert broadcasting, case-related messaging, and a system-wide audit trail, the platform seeks to improve the efficiency, transparency, and accountability of existing rescue operations. Ultimately, the study demonstrates how technology, when guided by ethical principles and evaluated using recognized standards such as ISO/IEC 25010:2023 and the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), can contribute meaningfully to animal welfare, public health, community participation, and local governance. REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES Related Literatures The theoretical and statutory foundations of community-based animal welfare and zoonotic disease control in the Philippines are anchored in an evolving legal framework that increasingly recognizes the interconnectedness of animal welfare, public 

=== PDF PAGE 6 ===
 health, and community responsibility. In recent years, the legal treatment of animals has undergone a significant paradigm shift from a protection-oriented perspective toward a welfare-centered approach grounded in the concept of animal sentience. Senate Bill No. 1684 (Senate of the Philippines, 2026) formally recognizes animals as sentient beings capable of experiencing both positive and negative affective states. Building upon this principle, House Bill No. 1746 (House of Representatives, 2025) adopts the internationally recognized Five Domains of Animal Welfare as the benchmark for humane treatment, while proposing the establishment of an Animal Welfare Bureau, legal protections for volunteer rescuers, and the implementation of Trap-Neuter-Vaccinate-Return (TNVR) programs as a humane population-control strategy. Complementing these reforms, Senate Bill No. 1276 (Senate of the Philippines, 2025) outlines species-specific and context-sensitive welfare standards that strengthen local enforcement mechanisms and ensure continuity with existing animal welfare legislation. Collectively, these measures reflect the Philippine government's increasing commitment to institutionalizing animal welfare through proactive governance and community participation. Beyond the Philippine context, similar developments have emerged internationally. The United Kingdom's Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act of 2022 (UK Parliament, 2022) formally embedded animal sentience into domestic law by recognizing the capacity of animals to experience feelings and by establishing an Animal Sentience Committee tasked with reviewing government policies that affect animal welfare. Providing the theoretical foundation for such reforms, Robertson and Goldsworthy (2022) argued in the Monash University Law Review that the Five Domains Model should serve as the basis of meaningful legal recognition of animal sentience, emphasizing that animal welfare frameworks should actively promote positive welfare outcomes rather than merely prevent suffering. Together, these international developments demonstrate a growing global consensus that animal welfare governance must extend beyond cruelty prevention and toward the creation of systems 

=== PDF PAGE 7 ===
 that actively support animal well-being. Such principles reinforce the need for community-based technological interventions capable of supporting welfare initiatives at the local level. While animal welfare legislation establishes the ethical and legal basis for intervention, public health frameworks provide an equally important justification for managing stray animal populations. The Department of Health's standardized first-aid guidelines (Bobis, 2024) emphasize the importance of immediate wound management and post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) following animal bites or scratches, while discouraging ineffective traditional treatments that often delay professional medical care. Reinforcing these efforts, House Bill No. 496 (House of Representatives, 2024) seeks to institutionalize the provision of free anti-rabies vaccines nationwide, thereby improving accessibility to life-saving treatment and reducing disparities in healthcare access. These initiatives underscore the continuing public health risks associated with uncontrolled stray animal populations and highlight the need for systems that facilitate timely reporting and intervention. Because a community-based reporting platform such as STRAY-SAFE necessarily collects and processes residents' personal information alongside precise geolocation data, its design must also be examined against prevailing data protection statutes. Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the Data Privacy Act of 2012, establishes the legal obligations of both government and private entities engaged in the processing of personal data, grants data subjects the rights to be informed, to access, to correct, and to object to the processing of their information, and creates the National Privacy Commission as the primary regulatory body tasked with overseeing compliance. This statute directly informs the confidentiality, informed-consent, and role-based access-control safeguards that STRAY-SAFE must implement, given its reliance on resident-submitted location data, photographic evidence, and pet ownership records. 

=== PDF PAGE 8 ===
 To translate these welfare and public health objectives into effective technological solutions, appropriate evaluation frameworks are required. The ISO/IEC 25010:2023 software quality model (International Organization for Standardization, 2023) establishes internationally recognized criteria for evaluating software systems in terms of functional suitability, performance efficiency, usability, reliability, security, compatibility, maintainability, and portability. However, the success of a technology-driven intervention depends not only on technical quality but also on user acceptance. Al-Emran and Granić (2021), through a bibliometric analysis of 2,399 articles on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) published between 2010 and 2020, examined whether the model remains valid for explaining the determinants of users' technology adoption behavior, with Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU) and Perceived Usefulness (PU) serving as its core constructs. These findings provide a theoretical basis for evaluating whether a dedicated stray animal reporting system can achieve meaningful community adoption compared with existing social media-based reporting practices. The effectiveness of community-based reporting platforms further depends on the active participation of citizens. Grounding the system's crowdsourcing architecture in established public health practice, Atienza et al. (2023) provided evidence-based guidelines for building citizen-led, geographically linked data collection platforms for disease surveillance and public health advocacy. Their findings indicate that successful crowdsourcing initiatives require reliable geospatial infrastructure, sustained community engagement, and a stable organizational framework. These principles align closely with the multi-role reporting architecture of STRAY-SAFE and support the use of citizens as active contributors to community-level monitoring and response systems. Recent advancements in artificial intelligence have likewise expanded the capabilities of digital reporting platforms. In particular, developments in computer vision have enabled the automation of image-based classification tasks relevant to 

=== PDF PAGE 9 ===
 community rescue and monitoring systems. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have consistently demonstrated high levels of accuracy in object detection, image recognition, and species classification applications. Ibraheam et al. (2023) demonstrated an accurate and computationally efficient animal species detection system suitable for deployment on embedded and resource-constrained devices, highlighting the potential of AI-assisted recognition systems to reduce reliance on manual image assessment even under variable hardware conditions. Building upon these advances, the YOLO (You Only Look Once) family of object detection architectures emerged as a leading framework for real-time image analysis due to its ability to process entire images in a single forward pass. This capability enables rapid and efficient classification, making YOLO particularly suitable for time-sensitive operational environments. Through a comprehensive comparative review of YOLO architectures from YOLOv1 to YOLOv8 and YOLO-NAS, Terven et al. (2023) concluded that YOLOv8 offers superior detection accuracy, faster inference speed, and a more flexible training pipeline than its predecessors. Developed by Ultralytics (Jocher et al., 2023), YOLOv8 is particularly suitable for deployment in community-based reporting systems where image quality, environmental conditions, and user hardware vary considerably. These characteristics make YOLOv8 an appropriate architecture for systems that require automated classification of user-submitted animal images within real-world reporting environments. Beyond species classification, computer vision techniques have also been applied to the more specific problem of reuniting lost pets with their owners. Voinea et al. (2023) introduced a contrastive learning-based transformer model capable of distinguishing between individual pet images with a reported test accuracy of ninety percent, demonstrating the technical feasibility of AI-assisted visual matching as a foundation for lost-pet identification applications. This finding lends further technical support to the integration of an AI-assisted matching mechanism within STRAY-SAFE for comparing images of reported stray animals against registered lost-pet profiles. 

=== PDF PAGE 10 ===
 In addition to artificial intelligence, Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and spatial analytics have become indispensable tools in public health surveillance, emergency response planning, and community-level resource allocation. Through a comprehensive review of GIS applications in public health surveillance, Biu et al. (2024) established that spatial data systems enable authorities to monitor disease distribution, identify geographic clusters of concern, and support evidence-based decision-making beyond what traditional tabular records can provide. Similarly, Sambo et al. (2024) demonstrated that geographic and spatial factors significantly influence the completeness and coverage of dog vaccination campaigns against rabies, underscoring the value of spatial mapping in identifying underserved high-risk communities and directing vaccination resources toward areas most in need of intervention. These findings establish a strong academic foundation for applying geospatial technologies to community-based animal welfare and public health initiatives. By transforming resident-generated reports into actionable geospatial intelligence, heatmap-based visualization systems can support evidence-based decision-making regarding vaccine allocation, sterilization campaigns, hotspot identification, and the strategic deployment of rescue resources. Beyond technical and spatial considerations, the accountability of digital platforms deployed within Philippine local government structures rests on the broader governance principles of transparency and accountability. Castillo and Gabriel (2020) explain that transparency embraces accountability: opening government operations to public scrutiny and citizen participation is meaningful only insofar as it holds government personnel to account, and the effectiveness of transparency is therefore measured by the degree of accountability it provides. In the context of STRAY-SAFE, this underscores the relevance of embedding a system-wide audit logging mechanism so that user actions, report updates, and administrative decisions remain transparent and traceable throughout the reporting and rescue workflow.

 Related Studies Empirical studies both locally and internationally demonstrate the growing effectiveness of digital technologies in improving animal welfare management, disease surveillance, and rescue coordination. Within the Philippine context, several studies have focused on digitizing companion animal services and shelter operations. Sarmiento et al. (2025) developed PawFect Home, a web-based reporting, sheltering, and adoption platform evaluated using ISO 25010 and TAM constructs, while Landicho et al. (2025) introduced BOOP, a mobile application that consolidates pet-care services including adoption, grooming, and boarding. Similarly, Gatmaitan et al. (2024) validated the effectiveness of the iPET web application in replacing paper-based shelter tracking with organized digital profiles. Complementing these efforts, Ganiron et al. (2026) proposed Care for Paws, an integrated Philippine information system that uses scannable identification tags to allow individuals who encounter a lost or unattended pet to access its profile and contact its owner, while also supporting vaccination and medication tracking for registered animals. Collectively, these studies demonstrate the growing effectiveness of digital platforms in improving animal management, adoption coordination, shelter administration, scannable pet identification, and information accessibility. However, their primary focus remains companion animal services rather than community-based rescue reporting and emergency response coordination. Complementing these companion-animal platforms, the AI-assisted visual matching approach demonstrated by Voinea et al. (2023) and the scannable-tag identification approach demonstrated by Ganiron et al. (2026) together substantiate the practical feasibility of an automated matching mechanism between reported strays and registered pets. These findings support the practical value of embedding a scannable pet-identification and matching feature, such as the Pet QR Registration and Management module, within a community-based rescue platform such as STRAY-SAFE.

 Beyond companion animal management, several studies have highlighted the importance of data-driven approaches to zoonotic disease surveillance and public health intervention. Evangelio (2024) developed RabDash DC, a web-based rabies analytics dashboard that integrates epidemiological forecasting and spatial data visualization for disease monitoring in Davao City. Supporting this need for stronger surveillance systems, Rysava et al. (2022) found that fragmented reporting infrastructures and disconnected datasets contribute to delayed outbreak detection and preventable rabies-related deaths. Similarly, Formstone et al. (2026) identified operational bottlenecks in canine-mediated rabies outbreak management, while Roldan-Gan and Gregorio (2025) reported persistent weaknesses in local implementation arising from poor intersectoral coordination and insufficient prioritization of One Health initiatives. Collectively, these studies underscore the continuing challenges associated with fragmented reporting systems, limited coordination, and insufficient data integration within local governance structures. Studies on barangay-level digital reporting further illustrate the applicability of structured, multi-tiered workflows to Philippine local governance contexts beyond animal welfare specifically. Laurena et al. (2022) developed AidPack, a web-based report management system that allows residents to submit incident reports directly to barangay officials, incorporating a geospatial heatmap for monitoring report distribution and an announcement feature for official communication back to residents. This study affirms the feasibility and practical benefit of digitizing citizen-to-barangay reporting channels, lending empirical support to the multi-tiered validation-to-dispatch workflow structured within STRAY-SAFE, wherein a report moves from citizen submission through subdivision-level validation and escalation to barangay-level dispatch and resolution. Research focusing on rescue coordination and intelligent reporting systems further demonstrates the value of integrating digital technologies into animal welfare 

=== PDF PAGE 13 ===
 operations. Efe et al. (2025), through the Be My Home platform, demonstrated that location-based filtering and spatial databases can significantly improve rescue response efficiency within smart city environments. Similarly, Kannan et al. (2026) developed ResQNet, a web platform through which citizens submit stray-dog reports that are screened by a convolutional neural network on the uploaded images and routed, based on the reporter's location, to the nearest registered NGO, which is then notified automatically. A/P Subramaniam and Kutty Mammi (2025) likewise developed the Stray Rescue Management System (SRMS), a web-based platform that integrates stray-animal reporting, adoption management, volunteer coordination, and donation handling. While these systems demonstrate the effectiveness of location-based technologies, AI-assisted reporting, and coordinated rescue workflows, none fully integrates AI-powered species identification, duplicate report detection, heatmap analytics, and barangay-level governance structures within a single platform designed for Philippine local communities. Synthesis The reviewed literature establishes that effective stray animal management requires the integration of animal welfare governance, public health intervention, citizen participation, artificial intelligence, and geospatial technologies. Legislative frameworks emphasize the growing recognition of animal welfare as a societal responsibility, while public health literature highlights the continuing threat posed by zoonotic diseases such as rabies. Complementing these foundations, studies on crowdsourcing, software quality evaluation, technology acceptance, computer vision, and geospatial analytics demonstrate how digital technologies can support more efficient reporting, monitoring, and decision-making processes at the community level. In addition, data privacy statutes and public administration literature on transparency and accountability establish the legal and governance basis for a multi-tiered, auditable reporting workflow, while studies on lost-pet matching systems, scannable pet-identification platforms, and 

=== PDF PAGE 14 ===
 barangay-level digital complaint systems affirm the feasibility of the QR-based pet recovery mechanism and the validation-to-dispatch escalation workflow that STRAY-SAFE proposes to implement. The reviewed studies further demonstrate the practical viability of technology-driven solutions in improving animal management, rescue coordination, and disease surveillance elsewhere: existing systems have successfully digitized shelter operations, enhanced adoption management, improved epidemiological monitoring, supported scannable pet identification, and reduced manual complaint-processing workload in barangay settings. This body of evidence indicates that the individual mechanisms STRAY-SAFE proposes to adopt — AI-assisted classification and matching, geospatial heatmapping, QR-based identification, and digitized citizen-to-government escalation — have each produced positive operational outcomes in comparable deployments. It does not, by itself, constitute proof that STRAY-SAFE will produce the same outcomes once deployed in Barangay San Vicente; that determination is the specific purpose of the study's own Technology Acceptance Model and ISO/IEC 25010:2023 evaluation described in Chapter 2, which is designed to generate the empirical evidence of the system's actual usefulness and quality that the reviewed literature, on its own, cannot provide. Despite the demonstrated viability of these individual components, significant gaps remain in their integration within a single platform tailored to the governance realities of Philippine barangays. Existing solutions generally focus on isolated functions such as adoption management, disease surveillance, lost-pet recovery, or general community complaint-handling, leaving unresolved issues related to duplicate report management, barangay-level rescue coordination, and unified operational workflows purpose-built for stray animal management. It is on this basis, rather than on any single novel algorithm or technique, that STRAY-SAFE's contribution rests: none of the reviewed AI, geospatial, pet-identification, or governance components is itself new, but their 

=== PDF PAGE 15 ===
 combination into one platform, purpose-built around the citizen-subdivision-barangay-administrator hierarchy of Philippine local governance, has not been demonstrated in the reviewed literature. STRAY-SAFE's novelty is therefore integrative and contextual — the localization and unification of proven mechanisms for a specific governance setting — rather than technical in the sense of introducing a new model, algorithm, or method. These identified gaps provide the rationale for the development of STRAY-SAFE. The proposed system addresses the limitations of existing approaches by integrating crowdsourced reporting, AI-assisted image classification and lost-pet matching, geospatial heatmap analytics, duplicate report detection, QR-based pet identification, a multi-tiered validation-escalation-dispatch workflow, audit-trail accountability, and multi-level stakeholder coordination into a unified web-based platform specifically designed for Philippine local government operations. In doing so, STRAY-SAFE seeks to transform fragmented social media-based reporting into a structured, data-driven, and privacy-conscious mechanism intended to enhance animal welfare, strengthen public health surveillance, improve rescue coordination, and support evidence-based decision-making at the community level — an intended benefit that the study's evaluation phase is designed to confirm. SCOPES AND DELIMITATION Scope The scope of STRAY-SAFE encompasses the design, development, and evaluation of a comprehensive system for community-based animal welfare and stray animal rescue management. The system is composed of a web-based platform that supports four primary user roles: Administrators, Barangay Staff/Rescuers, Subdivision Leaders, and Residents/Citizens. Each user role is provided with specific modules and functionalities according to their responsibilities within the platform. Development is limited to web-based front-end and back-end frameworks, a cloud-hosted relational database, AI-assisted image classification, and geolocation-mapping services appropriate for a community-scale deployment. The scope further includes the validation of the system's Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU) and Perceived Usefulness (PU) based on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), and the evaluation of its software quality based on the ISO/IEC 25010:2023 standard, specifically covering Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Usability, Reliability, and Security.

Theoretical Framework The development and evaluation of STRAY-SAFE: Smart Stray Animal Reporting and Rescue System is grounded in established theories and frameworks that explain technology adoption, software quality, public health intervention, and community participation. These theories provide the conceptual basis for understanding how the proposed system can be accepted by users, evaluated for quality, and utilized as a tool for improving community-based stray animal management. The primary theoretical foundation of this study is the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) developed by Fred Davis in 1989. TAM explains how users come to accept and utilize new technologies. According to the model, an individual's intention to use a system is primarily influenced by two factors: Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU) and Perceived Usefulness (PU). Perceived Ease of Use refers to the degree to which an individual believes that using a system will require minimal effort, while Perceived Usefulness refers to the extent to which an individual believes that the system will improve task performance. Within the context of STRAY-SAFE, TAM provides the basis for evaluating whether citizens, subdivision leaders, and barangay personnel are willing to adopt a specialized reporting platform instead of relying on informal social media channels. The framework is particularly relevant because user acceptance directly 

=== PDF PAGE 33 ===
 influences the effectiveness and sustainability of any community-based information system. Complementing TAM is the ISO/IEC 25010:2023 Software Quality Model, which serves as the primary framework for evaluating the technical quality of the developed system. ISO/IEC 25010 establishes internationally recognized standards for assessing software products based on characteristics such as Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Usability, Reliability, Security, Compatibility, Maintainability, and Portability. In this study, the framework provides objective criteria for determining whether STRAY-SAFE meets acceptable software quality standards. By incorporating ISO/IEC 25010 into the evaluation process, the study moves beyond user perception and examines the technical performance and dependability of the system. The multi-tiered validation, escalation, and audit-trail mechanisms embedded within STRAY-SAFE's Scope are likewise anchored in the Security and Reliability characteristics of the ISO/IEC 25010:2023 model, which require that a system's operations be accountable, traceable, and appropriately access-controlled across all user roles. This requirement is reinforced by Republic Act No. 10173, the Data Privacy Act of 2012, which obligates the system to safeguard the personal information and geolocation data it collects through defined access controls, informed consent, and accountability mechanisms, and by public administration literature on transparency and accountability in Philippine local governance, which underscores the value of structured audit trails in strengthening public trust in digitally-mediated government processes. Taken together, these standards and statutes provide the governance-oriented counterpart to TAM's user-acceptance focus: while TAM evaluates whether residents, subdivision leaders, and barangay personnel find the reporting and escalation workflow easy to use and useful, the Security and Reliability characteristics of ISO/IEC 25010, read alongside RA 10173 and the local-governance accountability literature, evaluate whether that same workflow safeguards personal data and preserves an auditable record of 

=== PDF PAGE 34 ===
 administrative action — both of which are necessary conditions for STRAY-SAFE to function as a trustworthy extension of barangay authority rather than merely a convenient reporting tool. The study is further supported by the One Health Framework, an interdisciplinary approach that recognizes the interconnected relationship among human health, animal health, and environmental health. The framework emphasizes that health challenges affecting one domain often have consequences for the others. In the context of stray animal management, uncontrolled stray populations may contribute to the spread of zoonotic diseases such as rabies, increase environmental sanitation concerns, and create risks to public safety. By facilitating the reporting, monitoring, and management of stray animal incidents through a web-based platform, STRAY-SAFE contributes to the broader objectives of the One Health Framework by supporting preventive public health measures and promoting responsible animal welfare practices. Another theoretical foundation relevant to this study is Crowdsourcing Theory, which explains how collective participation from members of the public can be harnessed to generate useful information and solve community problems. Crowdsourcing relies on the principle that large groups of individuals can contribute valuable data that may not be obtainable through traditional institutional methods alone. Within STRAY-SAFE, residents serve as community-based information sources by submitting reports, images, and geolocation data regarding stray animal incidents. These contributions enable local authorities and rescuers to receive timely information, improve situational awareness, and respond more effectively to community concerns. The theory therefore supports the system's citizen-centered reporting architecture and validates the role of residents as active participants in public service delivery. Collectively, these theories and frameworks establish the intellectual foundation of the study. Gu (2025) discussed React-based web applications and identified performance considerations such as component re-rendering, state management, and user interface responsiveness, supporting the use of React for interactive web application development.  Server-side operations are handled by a FastAPI backend written in Python, which manages API requests, business logic, system workflows, and communication with the database. The system uses JSON Web Tokens (JWT) together with HttpOnly cookies for authenticated sessions. Passwords are securely hashed, while token revocation, rate limiting, and scope validation are applied to strengthen access control and protect system resources. Database records are managed through MySQL and SQLAlchemy, with the database deployed through Aiven. The participants are selected based on their relevance to the development and evaluation of the STRAY-SAFE system. The final sample size for the qualitative informants is determined through data saturation, wherein interviews continue until no substantially new themes or insights emerge from the collected data (Braun & Clarke, 2006). Quantitative Respondents The quantitative respondents consist of residents of the Selera Homes Subdivision, who serve as the primary reporting source and general end users of the STRAY-SAFE web application. Residents were included as quantitative respondents on the basis of the following criteria: (1) they are verified, current residents of the selected deployment subdivision in Santa Maria, Bulacan; (2) they possess personal experience with stray animal encounters within the community; and (3) they own or have regular access to an internet-enabled device with camera capability sufficient to access the STRAY-SAFE web application and submit reports.
