CHAPTER 1
PROJECT RATIONALE
Introduction
Approximately four million animal bite cases are recorded every year in the
Philippines (Herriman, 2026), and 13 of the 17 rabies cases recorded by the Department
of Health from January 4 to February 21, 2026 came from unvaccinated animals, with
cases attributed almost equally to pets and stray animals (Banal, 2026). Although the
Animal Welfare Act of 1998 (Republic Act No. 8485), as amended by Republic Act No.
10631, provides a legal framework for the humane treatment and control of animals,
communities still report stray animals through informal channels such as Facebook
posts and Messenger group chats. These channels cannot record, prioritize, or track a
case from report to rescue, so without a centralized reporting system the risk posed by
stray animals cannot be managed effectively.
The same risk is present in Bulacan, where the Bulacan Provincial Health Office
recorded 53,013 animal bite cases and 24 rabies deaths in 2023, and Santa Maria
recorded three rabies cases (Silverio, 2024). Barangay San Vicente, the third most
populous of Santa Maria's 24 barangays and classified as urban, had 33,691 residents in
the 2024 census (Philippine Statistics Authority [PSA], 2025), and its subdivisions and
housing developments increase contact between residents and stray animals.
Preliminary interviews with the Barangay Captain, barangay staff, and subdivision
leaders, conducted before system development, confirmed that reports still travel
through Facebook posts and Messenger group chats. The interviews identified delayed
response times, reports that are lost in message threads, duplicate reports of the same
animal, and no historical record of incidents or rescue outcomes. Without a centralized
system, officials cannot see where incidents occur, which areas are at risk, or whether a
report was resolved, and citizens receive little feedback on what happened to their

To address the observed problems, this study proposes STRAYSAFE, a web-
based platform that replaces informal reporting with a structured workflow. Where
reports are scattered across chat threads, STRAYSAFE collects each report in one form
with photos, location, and animal details, and automated image recognition helps check
species, photo authenticity, and duplicates. Where responses are delayed, each report
moves through subdivision-level validation and barangay-level dispatch with near-real-
time status tracking and notifications. Where no records exist, case histories, heatmaps,
and an audit trail give officials the data needed for decisions and accountability. The
platform also supports pet QR registration, holding facility monitoring, and adoption
management after rescue.
In direct contribution to local government operations, STRAYSAFE offers a
faster, more transparent, and more accountable way to manage stray animal incidents. It
supports Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 3, Good Health and Well-being, by
enabling timely reporting of incidents that may pose rabies and other zoonotic risks,

Related Literatures
This section reviews the legal, public health, technological, and governance
concepts that underpin STRAYSAFE: animal welfare legislation, the One Health

image recognition, geospatial analytics, and accountability in local government

Animal Welfare Legislation and Governance
The theoretical and statutory foundations of community-based animal welfare
and zoonotic disease control in the Philippines are anchored in an evolving legal
framework that increasingly recognizes the interconnectedness of animal welfare,
public health, and community responsibility. In recent years, the legal treatment of
animals has shifted from a protection-oriented perspective toward a welfare-centered
approach grounded in animal sentience. Senate Bill No. 1684 (Senate of the Philippines,
2026) formally recognizes animals as sentient beings capable of experiencing positive
and negative affective states. Building on this principle, House Bill No. 1746 (House of
Representatives, 2025) adopts the internationally recognized Five Domains of Animal
Welfare as the benchmark for humane treatment, while proposing an Animal Welfare
Bureau, legal protections for volunteer rescuers, and Trap-Neuter-Vaccinate-Return
(TNVR) programs as a humane population-control strategy. Complementing these
reforms, Senate Bill No. 1276 (Senate of the Philippines, 2025) outlines species-

governance must extend beyond cruelty prevention toward systems that actively
support animal well-being, and they reinforce the need for community-based
technological interventions at the local level.

Citizen Participation and Crowdsourcing
The effectiveness of community-based reporting platforms further depends on
citizen participation. Atienza et al. (2023), in "Fourteen Quick Tips for Crowdsourcing

reliable geospatial infrastructure, sustained community engagement, and a stable
organizational framework. These principles align with the multi-role reporting
architecture of STRAYSAFE and support the use of citizens as active contributors to
community-level monitoring and response.

Geospatial Analytics and Heatmap Visualization

response planning, and community-level resource allocation. Biu et al. (2024), in "A

Accountability in Local Government Reporting
Finally, the accountability of digital platforms deployed within Philippine local
government structures depends on traceable records and clear communication between
citizens and authorities. Laurena et al. (2022), in "AidPack: A Web-Based Report
Management System for Community Incident Response," showed that a barangay-level
report management system with an announcement feature allows residents to submit
incident reports directly to officials and receive official communication in return, which
improves visibility of how reports are handled. Rysava et al. (2022), in "One Health
Surveillance for Rabies: A Case Study of Integrated Bite Case Management in Albay
Province, Philippines," found that fragmented reporting infrastructures and
disconnected datasets contribute to delayed outbreak detection and preventable rabies-
related deaths, while Atienza et al. (2023) emphasized the need for a stable
organizational framework in citizen-led data platforms. Taken together, these studies
highlight the value of structured case records and role-based action histories. In the
context of STRAYSAFE, this supports a system-wide audit trail so that user actions,
report updates, and administrative decisions remain traceable throughout the reporting
and rescue workflow.

requires the integration of animal welfare governance, public health intervention,

frameworks emphasize animal welfare as a societal responsibility, while public health
literature highlights the continuing threat of zoonotic diseases such as rabies. Studies on
crowdsourcing, software quality evaluation, technology acceptance, automated image
recognition, and geospatial analytics show how digital technologies can support more
efficient reporting, monitoring, and decision-making at the community level. In

STATEMENT OF THE PROBLEM
Barangay San Vicente, Santa Maria, Bulacan needs a centralized system that
addresses fragmented and informal stray animal reporting. Reports sent through
Facebook posts and Messenger group chats can be delayed, lost, or duplicated, and they
leave no historical record of how a case was handled, as the preliminary interviews with
the Barangay Captain, barangay staff, and subdivision leaders also showed. To address
this problem, the study proposes STRAYSAFE, or Smart Stray Animal Reporting and
Rescue System, a web application for residents, subdivision leaders, barangay
personnel, and system administrators. It gives residents a structured way to report stray
animals and follow their reports, helps subdivision leaders validate reports, helps
barangay personnel dispatch and document rescues and manage animals after rescue,
and gives administrators system-wide oversight. STRAYSAFE aims to make stray
animal reporting more organized, transparent, and accountable while keeping validation,

SCOPES AND DELIMITATION
Scope
The scope of STRAYSAFE is presented by module rather than by type of user.
Each module lists its submodules and states what each authorized user can view or do
within them. Role-based permissions control access to every module and submodule,
and a user role is named only where its function or the information it receives differs.
STRAYSAFE is a web application for community-based stray animal reporting
and rescue management at the subdivision and barangay level. It will be piloted in
Selera Homes Subdivision, Barangay San Vicente, Santa Maria, Bulacan. The system
supports four user roles: Residents/Citizens, who report and track incidents;
Subdivision Leaders, who validate and escalate reports; Barangay Staff (barangay
personnel, including field rescue team members), who finalize priority, dispatch rescues,
and manage the holding facility and adoptions; and System Administrators, who
provide system-wide oversight. A limited set of public functions is open without an
account: scanning a pet QR tag, browsing the adoption catalog, and verifying an
adoption certificate.
Development is limited to web-based front-end and back-end frameworks, a
cloud-hosted relational database, automated image recognition (cat-or-dog species
identification, duplicate report detection, pet matching, and photo authenticity checking,
referred to in the system as AI-assisted features) used as decision-support information,
and geolocation and mapping services suited to a community-scale deployment. The

Delimitation
The study does not include veterinary diagnosis, animal health assessment,
automated treatment recommendations, advanced breed identification, or automated
decisions on euthanasia or legal penalties. Care notes and condition observations
entered by staff are manual, non-clinical records and are not veterinary assessments.
Owner warnings issued by the system are record-keeping notices and do not impose
fines or legal penalties. The automated image recognition component is limited to
animal detection, cat-or-dog species identification, photo authenticity checking, image
similarity checking and pet matching, and duplicate report detection. Its outputs are
decision-support information that users review and confirm. Report priority is assigned
by the system from predefined severity criteria: Subdivision Leaders review and
recommend a priority level, Barangay Staff finalize it, and System Administrators
review it.
STRAYSAFE operates as a standalone web-based platform and does not
integrate with national identification databases, veterinary information systems,
banking APIs, or third-party rescue management platforms. The system does use third-
party cloud services for image analysis, media storage, email delivery, and map and
routing data, and these services are not integrations with external agency systems. The
Pet QR Code feature is intended solely for pet identification and ownership verification
and does not track the location of registered pets. Public access without an account is
limited to scanning a pet QR tag, browsing the adoption catalog, and verifying an
adoption certificate.

Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)
Davis's (1989) Technology Acceptance Model explains how users come to
accept and use new technologies. An individual's intention to use a system is influenced
mainly by Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU), the degree to which using the system
requires minimal effort, and Perceived Usefulness (PU), the extent to which the system
improves task performance. In STRAYSAFE, TAM provides the basis for evaluating
whether residents, subdivision leaders, and barangay personnel are willing to adopt a
dedicated reporting platform instead of informal social media channels.
ISO/IEC 25010:2023 Product Quality Model
ISO/IEC 25010:2023 (International Organization for Standardization, 2023) is
an evaluation standard rather than a behavioral theory. It defines nine product quality
characteristics: Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Compatibility,
Interaction Capability, Reliability, Security, Maintainability, Flexibility, and Safety.
Interaction Capability and Flexibility are the names the 2023 edition uses for the
characteristics previously called Usability and Portability, and Safety is a new
characteristic. IT experts assess all nine characteristics, while end users assess all
except Maintainability, which they are not in a position to judge.
One Health Framework
The One Health Framework is an interdisciplinary approach that recognizes the
interconnection of human, animal, and environmental health (Roldan-Gan & Gregorio,
2025; Formstone et al., 2026). It emphasizes that health challenges in one domain often
affect the others. In stray animal management, uncontrolled stray populations may
contribute to zoonotic disease spread, sanitation concerns, and risks to public safety. By
supporting the reporting, monitoring, and management of stray animal incidents,
STRAYSAFE contributes to the preventive and coordinated aims of this framework.

Crowdsourcing Theory
Crowdsourcing Theory explains how collective participation by members of the
public can generate useful information and help solve community problems, including
data that institutional methods alone may not obtain (Atienza et al., 2023). In
STRAYSAFE, residents act as community-based information sources by submitting
reports, images, and location data. This supports the citizen-centered reporting design
of the system and the role of residents as active participants in public service.
Data Privacy and Accountability
Republic Act No. 10173, the Data Privacy Act of 2012, requires the system to
safeguard the personal information and location data it collects through access controls,
informed consent, and accountability measures. Together with the Security and
Reliability characteristics of ISO/IEC 25010:2023 and studies on barangay-level digital
reporting (Laurena et al., 2022), it supports the role-based access, escalation records,
and system-wide audit trail of STRAYSAFE, so that the reporting workflow remains
traceable and trustworthy as an extension of barangay authority.

The Process Phase follows the Iterative Incremental Software Development
Model, in which the system is built and refined in repeated cycles, each adding a
working set of functions. Requirement analysis translates stakeholder needs into user

increment undergoes system testing to confirm that its functions work as specified.
After the final increment, system evaluation follows: end users rate Perceived Ease of
Use and Perceived Usefulness using TAM, and IT experts and end users rate software
quality using ISO/IEC 25010:2023, with end users excluding Maintainability. Ratings
are compared with the acceptance benchmark of 2.51 on the four-point scale. Findings
then drive iterative refinement, so that problems found in one cycle are corrected in the
next.

HYPOTHESIS OF THE STUDY
The following hypotheses evaluate the acceptability of STRAYSAFE among its
intended end users (residents, subdivision leaders, and barangay personnel, including
rescue team members):
● H₀: The mean Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU) and Perceived Usefulness (PU)
ratings of STRAYSAFE among end users are not significantly higher than the
acceptance benchmark of 2.51 on the four-point scale (μ ≤ 2.51).
● H₁: The mean PEOU and PU ratings of STRAYSAFE among end users are
significantly higher than 2.51 (μ > 2.51) at the 0.05 level of significance.
PEOU and PU are tested separately using a one-tailed, one-sample t-test, and
the null hypothesis is rejected for a construct only when the p-value is below 0.05 and
the sample mean is above 2.51. The 2.51 benchmark is the lower bound of the favorable
(Agree/Acceptable) interval on the four-point scale. The ISO/IEC 25010:2023 ratings
of the IT experts and of the end users are interpreted descriptively against the same
benchmark and are not treated as a separate inferential hypothesis, because the number
of IT experts is limited.
