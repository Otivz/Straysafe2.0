Complaint Management and progressive enforcement

One place for the Subdivision Leader to verify animal complaints, confirm who is responsible, see each person’s repeat history per rule, and track what the rules require: warning letters and their delivery, the office summons, the fine and the Barangay meeting. Complaints stay ordinary cases.

What StraySafe has today

Read from the code and the local database (read only) on 13 Nov 2026.

Already there, and reused

  • A complaint is already a case: “Animal complaint” files a nuisance case, “It may be dangerous” a safety case. Both are private.
  • The Leader’s assessment records whether the incident happened, with its basis (incident_verification).
  • The ownership review records a confirmed person with evidence kinds and a note.
  • Case links already know “same incident” and “duplicate”, and closing has a “duplicate report” outcome.
  • The Barangay referral log (statuses and dated updates), the office address in Settings, the case history and Leader notifications.

There, but it does not fit this policy

  • Warnings (owner_warnings) climb four tiers (Notice, 1st, 2nd, Final Notice) and count earlier warnings, not verified incidents.
  • Nuisance and safety are both saved as one violation type, so different rules are combined for the same person.
  • A warning needs a StraySafe account, and is deleted with that account (ON DELETE CASCADE).
  • “Delivered” today means the in-app notice at the moment of issuing.
  • The case page says “No fine is assessed by the subdivision”; the old fine handling was retired on 10 Nov.

Missing

  • A record for the person responsible that works without an account (a household without the app).
  • A place for the complainant’s suggestion of the owner.
  • Warning-letter wording, printing and delivery tracking; the office summons; fine imposed and paid; the Barangay meeting with attendance and outcome.
  • Rules themselves: the local database has no subdivision policy configured (0 policies, 0 warnings, 0 observations). A policy holds a title, reference, summary, the concerns it covers and an optional observation period; it has no offense steps and no fine amount.

Recommended design

Built on the existing case system. One Complaints workspace; one complaint page per case; separate verified incidents; history counted per person and per rule.

Built around what really happens

  • StraySafe searches and suggests; the Leader checks where it happened and decides.
  • Missing data never blocks the work: no account, no registered pet, no name, no description.
  • Photos stay optional. With none attached, the last step reminds the resident (a short video for barking, a stronger reminder for a found animal), never blocks, and stays quiet for a dangerous or hurt animal.
  • No forced order where real life has none: the person can be found in the same visit as the check, and entries take the real date and time.
  • Every step can go wrong the way it does in real life (nobody home, refused to sign, did not come, meeting postponed), and each has a next move. No dead ends.
  • Leads can be ruled out with a reason, and a wrong finding can be corrected later. The history keeps both; nothing is erased.
  • What the subdivision has not decided is left open, not invented.

One case system

  • A complaint is the existing case. The Complaints workspace is a view of complaint cases plus an index of people responsible. Nothing is copied; the same case still appears under Cases.
  • Complaint cases open a calm, one-way page like the approved Lost & Found page: what was reported, the findings, the history, the steps, the activity. The next step is shown; everything else stays folded.
  • Lost & Found and every other kind of case are not touched.

Three findings, each the Leader’s

  • What happened: the existing incident verification, with its basis.
  • Which rule: one confirmed subdivision rule. This is the “violation type” that history is counted by.
  • Who is responsible: “Verified Responsible Person — Confirmed by Leader”, with evidence. A resident with an account, or a household recorded by name and house address. The Pet Registry is never required.
  • The complainant’s suggestion is kept apart as “Reported / Suspected Owner — Unverified”. It is never counted, never shown to anyone else and never notified.

Finding the owner, even when the complainant doesn’t know

  • The complainant’s suggestion, looked up in your records: a name or a house address that matches a resident or a household on record.
  • The animal’s description and photo, compared with the Pet Registry: same kind, colour and size, owners living on the same street first. The report already has colour, size and breed fields, and the server’s photo comparison (used today for pet matching) can rank registered pets’ photos.
  • Earlier complaints at the same place about the same kind of animal, and who was confirmed then. Often the strongest lead.
  • Leads only, never proof. Many animals are not registered (a household without the app cannot register one), and addresses are typed freely, so a lookup can miss. With no lead, the Leader checks on the ground: the time it usually happens, the guardhouse or CCTV, the neighbours.
  • Today’s Leader app has a registry-suggestion panel in the assessment, but it is switched off on the live server and compares colour words only, so this is new work.

Counting, suggested by StraySafe, confirmed by the Leader

  • Offense number = 1 + the person’s earlier confirmed offenses under the same rule.
  • Only verified incidents with a confirmed person count. A different rule has its own count. One occurrence counts once.
  • StraySafe shows the likely stage with the incidents it counted and those it did not. The Leader confirms it, or records why it should not count. Only then are the stage’s steps added.

Same occurrence, counted once

  • When two complaints look like the same occurrence (same rule, same place, same morning), StraySafe asks the Leader on the later one.
  • “Same occurrence” links it with the existing “same incident” link and closes it as a duplicate. It stays visible on both complaints and both complainants get the outcome.
  • “Separate incident” keeps it apart; it counts only if verified on its own.

Steps record facts, never assumptions

  • Letters: prepared (printed), issued for delivery, delivered (when, received by, how known) or not delivered (why). Every attempt is kept. Printed is never delivered.
  • Summons: date and place written in the second letter; attended, did not attend, or a new date; what was agreed.
  • Fine: imposed (amount read from the confirmed rule; StraySafe never works one out), then paid (date, received by). No payments or receipts in StraySafe.
  • Barangay meeting: arranged (also written in the existing referral log), then held or not, who attended, the outcome.

Kept for good, and private

  • Offenses, steps and their events are append-only. Closing or reopening a complaint changes none of them; a correction is a new entry with a reason.
  • The person record is never deleted with an account, so history survives.
  • Letters never name the complainant. The complainant sees “verified, action taken”. The person responsible learns who complained only at a Barangay meeting the complainant agreed to attend.

Data changes, when you approve (one reviewed SQL migration, additions only)

New columns on reports

  • suspected_owner_name, suspected_owner_address, suspected_owner_details: the complainant’s optional lead, private.
  • violation_policy_id with the rule’s title and reference copied at the time: the rule this incident broke.
  • respondent_id: the person the Leader confirmed as responsible.

New tables

  • complaint_respondents: subdivision, optional resident account (kept when the account goes), name, house address, who recorded it.
  • complaint_offenses: one per counted complaint: person, rule, offense number as confirmed, by whom and when; a later correction (voided, with reason) is recorded, never deleted.
  • enforcement_steps: each step of an offense (first letter, second letter, summons, fine, Barangay meeting) and where it stands.
  • enforcement_step_events: append-only: every print, issue, delivery attempt, attendance, payment record and meeting outcome, with who and when.

Settings, and what is retired

  • Each rule in the existing case settings gains: what each offense requires (from your policy: 1st letter; 2nd letter and summons; 3rd fine and Barangay meeting), an optional fine amount, and an optional expiry. Stored with the settings; no migration.
  • On complaint cases, “Issue warning” and its tier ladder are replaced by the offense steps. Old warning records stay readable. No observation period is started.
  • Unchanged: Lost & Found, notices, alerts, matching, messaging, every other kind of case.

Questions for the subdivision

The design works without these answers, but these details decide wording, amounts and edge cases. None of them is guessed in the mockup.

  1. Rules. Which violation types are there, with their official wording and reference (house rules, deed restrictions, an ordinance)? None is configured in StraySafe yet.
  2. Fines. May the subdivision impose fines, or only the Barangay? What amount for each rule? StraySafe currently tells Leaders that no fine is assessed by the subdivision.
  3. How long offenses count. Do they ever stop counting (after some months), or count forever?
  4. Notice first? If two incidents happen before the first letter is delivered, is the second one the 2nd offense, or still part of the 1st?
  5. 4th offense and later. Repeat the 3rd-offense steps, or something else?
  6. Who is responsible. A person or a household? What if a tenant, a helper or a visitor had the animal?
  7. Delivery. Who may deliver (the Leader, the guard)? Who may receive (a household member)? What counts as proof (a signed receiving copy)? What happens after a failed attempt?
  8. Summons. What happens if the person does not come?
  9. The fine. How is the person told, who receives payment, and what happens if it is not paid? StraySafe would record only “paid on, received by”.
  10. Barangay meeting. Must the complainant agree to attend, since their name becomes known? What if they decline?
  11. The complainant’s view. Is “verified, action taken” the right amount to show them?
  12. A copy in the app. Should a person with a StraySafe account also see their letter in the app? It would never count as delivery.
  13. Dangerous animals. Do “It may be dangerous” reports about an owned animal belong here and count under the rules?
  14. Earlier paper records. May the Leader enter offenses from before StraySafe, so counting starts at the right stage?
  15. Corrections. Can a person contest a finding, and who decides? Proposed: the Leader records a correction with a reason; nothing is deleted.
  16. Letters. The official wording, letterhead and who signs.

Left out on purpose

Online payment, sending letters or SMS from StraySafe, automatic penalties, fixed waiting periods, a screen for the person responsible in the Resident app, and observation periods. Each can be proposed separately if the subdivision asks for it.
